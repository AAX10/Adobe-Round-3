# E-003 — Context-Variance Structural Risk (Chunk Fragmentation)

## Mechanism
Chunk-Based RAG Context Drop

## Concrete Claim

IF:
The entity's visible content heavily clusters its core service terms exclusively with local region terms, while lacking paragraphs that combine the core service with broader regions or persona-agnostic framing,

THEN:
There is a structural risk of retrieval variance, because chunk-based RAG systems may isolate local terms from the core service offering, causing the LLM to drop the source for adjacent conversational contexts.

## Evidence Needed

### To Confirm
- Fetch and parse all paragraph `<p>` tags from the target page.
- Identify paragraphs containing core service terms.
- Determine if core service terms co-occur exclusively alongside local geographic modifiers within those paragraphs.
- Verify the absence of any paragraph where the service is described neutrally or with global modifiers.

### To Reject or Narrow
- Core services appear in paragraphs without local modifiers.
- The sample size of paragraphs is too small to establish a pattern.

## Observable Signal
Co-occurrence analysis in paragraphs reveals core entity terms appearing exclusively alongside local geographic terms, and never independently.

## Automated Decision Boundary
A finding is generated when:
1. Multiple paragraphs contain both service terms and local modifiers.
2. Zero paragraphs contain service terms independently or with global modifiers.

## Potential Severity

**Medium**: High inferred structural risk, but depends heavily on the specific RAG chunking strategy employed by the downstream AI.

## Potential Remediation
Create distinct paragraphs or standalone sentences that define the core service independent of local geographic modifiers.
- **Where to change:** Throughout the primary visible text of the homepage or service pages.
- **Example Fix:** Ensure that somewhere on the page, the service is described objectively. "Acme provides enterprise cloud security software." (No local modifiers).
- **Why this works:** When a RAG system chunks the page, it will yield at least one chunk that is purely topical and region-agnostic. This chunk acts as a safe retrieval anchor for broad, non-localized queries, preventing the LLM from dropping the source due to perceived local irrelevance.

## Current Evidence

**SOURCE-DERIVED FACT**: RAG splits pages into chunks. Dense retrieval scores chunks independently.

**OUR INFERENCE**: If core service definitions are inextricably bound to local terms in every chunk, any chunk retrieved for a generic query will carry heavy local bias. The LLM may subsequently discard the chunk as irrelevant to a non-local user.

## Counterexamples

1. **Service described neutrally in bullet points** → NOT a finding. If the structure breaks the pattern outside `<p>` tags, the chunking might still capture it. (Our check specifically targets `<p>` tags as the most common chunk unit).

## References

1. **Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering (Karpukhin et al., 2020)**
   - Organization: ACL / arXiv
   - URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906
   - Mechanism supported: Dense Retrieval Chunking
   - Claim supported: Passage embeddings are highly sensitive to the exact semantic composition of the text chunk; lack of overlapping generic terms reduces retrieval likelihood for generic queries.

## Known Limitations
- RAG chunking strategies vary wildly (sentence-level, paragraph-level, fixed token count). A paragraph-level check is a strong heuristic but not an absolute guarantee of how a specific model will chunk the text.
