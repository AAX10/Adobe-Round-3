# E-001 — Contextual Breadth & Entity Anchors

## Mechanism
Dense Retrieval / Embedding Spatial Bias

## Concrete Claim

IF:
An entity's primary homepage or about-page definition (e.g., meta description, main H1, leading paragraph) relies exclusively on local geographical or niche persona identifiers without a broader, persona-neutral and region-agnostic fallback statement,

THEN:
AI retrieval systems are more likely to drop this source for generalized or out-of-region queries because the semantic embedding vector is overly biased toward the narrow local context rather than the core entity concept.

## Evidence Needed

### To Confirm
- Fetch the homepage or "About" page of the entity.
- Extract the primary defining elements: `<title>`, `<meta name="description">`, `<h1>`, and the first `<p>`.
- Tokenize and count local location markers (cities, neighborhoods) versus generalized markers (state, country, generic service categories).
- Verify the presence of local markers and the complete absence of broader semantic anchors.

### To Reject or Narrow
- The page prominently includes state-level, national, or broad categorical qualifiers alongside the local ones.
- The entity is explicitly a single-location brick-and-mortar store with no online or multi-region services.

## Observable Signal
Count and ratio of local location markers versus generalized markers in the primary identifying text is heavily skewed towards local, with zero global anchors.

## Automated Decision Boundary
A finding is generated when:
1. The primary definition text contains multiple local geo-markers, AND
2. The primary definition text contains strictly zero higher-level geographic markers or generic entity markers.

## Potential Severity

**High**: High likelihood of discoverability failure for out-of-region conversational contexts. The source will systematically drop from generalized queries.

## Potential Remediation
Add a concise, persona-neutral, and region-agnostic entity definition that identifies the core service footprint. 
- **Where to change:** The `<meta name="description">` and the leading `<p>` tag on the homepage and "About" page.
- **Example Fix:** Instead of starting exclusively with *"We are Austin's best local neighborhood plumbers"*, update the text to: *"We are a plumbing service provider operating in the United States. We serve the greater Texas area, specializing as Austin's premier local plumber."*
- **Why this works:** It injects global semantic anchors ("plumbing service provider", "United States", "Texas") into the same embedding chunk as the local modifier, allowing the vector to overlap with generic queries while still satisfying local queries.

## Current Evidence

**SOURCE-DERIVED FACT**: Dense retrieval models (like those used in RAG systems) compute relevance by embedding queries and documents into a shared vector space. Texts dominated by specific modifiers (e.g., hyper-local city names) shift the document's vector away from the generalized concept centroid, resulting in lower dot-product similarity scores for generic queries.

**OUR INFERENCE**: If a page relies exclusively on hyper-local identifiers and lacks generic fallback definitions, it is structurally at risk of being bypassed by semantic search systems when the user's prompt context is broad or out-of-region.

## Counterexamples

1. **Explicitly local-only business** → NOT a finding if structured data (`areaServed`) explicitly restricts the business to the local coordinate bounding box.

## References

1. **Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering (Karpukhin et al., 2020)**
   - Organization: ACL / arXiv
   - URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906
   - Mechanism supported: Dense Retrieval Relevance
   - Claim supported: Passage embeddings are highly sensitive to the exact semantic composition of the text chunk; lack of overlapping generic terms reduces retrieval likelihood for generic queries.

## Known Limitations
- Determining what constitutes a "local" vs "global" marker deterministically requires high-quality NER (Named Entity Recognition) models; basic keyword matching can produce false positives or negatives.
- Some edge-case industries legitimately only define themselves locally.

## Experiment Design

1. **Condition tested**: Entity definition text lacks global/generic anchors.
2. **Expected bad outcome**: Source drops for out-of-region queries.
3. **Ground truth**: Manual review of the entity's actual service footprint vs its text.
4. **Supports hypothesis**: Text is highly localized while business is multi-regional.
5. **Kills hypothesis**: Text successfully retrieves for generic queries anyway due to downstream query expansion by the AI.
6. **Obvious false positives**: A purely local cafe.
7. **Automatable**: Yes, with NLP heuristics.
8. **Works on unseen site**: Yes.
9. **Evidence emitted**: Extracted text, local token counts, global token counts.
