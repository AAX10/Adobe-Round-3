# F-001 — Image-Only Core Facts

## Mechanism
lock-in

## Concrete Claim

IF:
an important factual claim (pricing, specifications, opening hours, product attributes, service details, or other key information central to the page's purpose) is communicated primarily through an image and no equivalent readable text representation exists in the page's HTML,

THEN:
a text-oriented machine reader may fail to reliably retrieve that fact, because it cannot parse the image's visual content and no textual fallback is available.

## Evidence Needed

### To Confirm
- Identify images on the page that appear to contain factual information (based on context: proximity to product/service content, alt text hints, surrounding headings)
- For each candidate factual image: check whether equivalent factual text exists in the page HTML (alt text, nearby text, captions, data attributes, JSON-LD, or any other machine-readable representation)
- Verify that the image contains genuinely important factual information (not decorative, not a logo where the brand name is in text)
- Demonstrate that a text-only extraction of the page would miss the fact

### To Reject or Narrow
- The image has comprehensive alt text that conveys the factual content → text equivalent exists
- The same factual information appears in nearby HTML text (caption, heading, paragraph, table) → equivalent text exists
- JSON-LD or Microdata provides the fact independently of the image → machine-readable representation exists
- The image is decorative, a logo (with textual brand name elsewhere), or a photograph without factual claims → not a factual image
- The image contains factual information that is also communicated through ARIA attributes → accessible equivalent exists

## Observable Signal
An image is identified in a factual content area, its alt text is empty/generic/missing, and no equivalent textual representation of the image's factual content exists in the surrounding HTML.

## Automated Decision Boundary
A finding is generated when:
1. An `<img>` element is identified within a factual content area (product section, specification area, pricing section), AND
2. The image's `alt` attribute is empty (`alt=""`), missing, or generic (e.g., "image", "photo", "icon"), AND
3. No `<figcaption>`, `<caption>`, or immediately adjacent descriptive text provides the equivalent factual information, AND
4. No JSON-LD or Microdata on the page provides the same fact, AND
5. The image's context suggests it contains important factual information (based on heading association, page type, and surrounding content patterns)

Note: This check uses deterministic signals (alt text, nearby text, JSON-LD) and does NOT require OCR or computer vision.

## Potential Severity

**High**: Multiple important facts on the page are locked in images with no text equivalent (e.g., an entire specification table as an image).

**Medium**: A few important facts are image-only.

**Low**: Supplementary information is image-only.

Severity depends on: importance of locked facts, number of affected facts, whether the facts are critical to the page's purpose.

## Potential Remediation
Provide equivalent readable text for every image that communicates important factual information. Options include: descriptive alt text containing the key facts, a visible text caption or adjacent paragraph, a data table replicating the image's factual content, or JSON-LD/Microdata encoding the facts independently.

## Current Evidence

**SOURCE-DERIVED FACT**: W3C WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.1.1 (Non-text Content) requires that all non-text content have a text alternative serving an equivalent purpose. This accessibility standard directly addresses the machine-readability of visual content.

**SOURCE-DERIVED FACT**: Google Search Central documentation recommends providing descriptive alt text for images and states that Googlebot uses alt text and surrounding text to understand image content.

**SOURCE-DERIVED FACT**: The Round 3 Handbook Section F explicitly describes content being "carried by something a summarizer can't read" as a cause of information loss.

**OUR INFERENCE**: When important facts exist only as visual content within images and no text equivalent is provided, text-oriented machine readers (including AI assistants that operate on page text) cannot reliably extract those facts.

**UNPROVEN DOWNSTREAM EFFECT**: Direct impact on AI citation is not established. Some AI systems have multimodal capabilities that could interpret images, but this cannot be assumed for all machine readers.

## Counterexamples

1. **Decorative images** → NOT a finding. Hero banners, background images, and aesthetic elements without factual content are not information lock-in.

2. **Logos where textual brand name exists** → NOT a finding. A logo image is not a factual lock-in if the brand name appears in text elsewhere on the page.

3. **Photographs without factual claims** → NOT a finding. A photo of a building or product is not locked-in factual information unless it communicates specific claims (dimensions, colors, features) not available in text.

4. **Images with comprehensive alt text** → NOT a finding. If `alt="Product X specifications: 15.6-inch display, 2.3 kg, 12-hour battery"` conveys the key facts, the text equivalent exists.

5. **Images alongside equivalent text** → NOT a finding. If a specification image is accompanied by a text table containing the same data, the facts are accessible.

## Known Limitations
- Cannot determine image content without OCR/computer vision (by design — uses deterministic signals)
- Relies on context heuristics (page area, heading association) to identify likely factual images
- Some AI systems have multimodal capabilities that could process images
- Alt text quality assessment (generic vs. descriptive) uses keyword heuristics
- Cannot exhaustively verify whether image content is truly factual vs. illustrative

## Experiment Design

1. **Condition tested**: Important facts locked in images without text equivalent
2. **Expected bad outcome**: Text-oriented machine reader cannot extract those facts
3. **Ground truth**: Manual identification of image-locked facts
4. **Supports hypothesis**: Important fact in image, no alt text, no nearby text, no JSON-LD
5. **Kills hypothesis**: All image facts have text equivalents (alt text, captions, data tables)
6. **Obvious false positives**: Decorative images, logos, photos, images with good alt text
7. **Automatable**: Yes — deterministic signal checking
8. **Works on unseen site**: Yes — image/alt-text analysis is universal
9. **Evidence emitted**: List of candidate factual images with alt text and nearby text analysis
